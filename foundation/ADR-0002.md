# ADR-0002 — Memory Atmosphere as a Governed Contextual Subsystem
**Status:** Accepted  
**Date:** 2026-07-24  
**Deciders:** Discovery Partner + Claude (architecture session)  
**Supersedes:** None  
**Superseded by:** None  
**Related documents:** MEMORY_ATMOSPHERE_ENGINE.md v0.1, AI_CONTEXT_BROKER.md v0.1, ARCHITECTURE_FREEZE_V1.md v1.1

---

## Context

LifeBook's memory-gathering experience requires an emotional and sensory environment that supports authentic recollection. As the product moves toward richer presentation layers — and as participant interviews begin — a foundational decision is needed about how that environment is governed.

Two inadequate framings have been explicitly rejected:

**Framing A — Background imagery:** The presentation layer selects images based on keywords extracted from the conversation. This approach is direct, technically simple, and wrong. It treats memory as indexable content rather than lived experience. It risks directing, biasing, or dramatizing recollection by placing a literal image in front of the participant before they have recalled it themselves. It collapses the distinction between what the participant said and what the system decided they meant.

**Framing B — Unconstrained generative atmosphere:** A generative model produces atmospheric visuals in response to conversational context. This approach is flexible but ungoverned. Without explicit constraints on specificity, sensitivity, authenticity, and consent, it creates imagery that may be vivid, culturally inappropriate, trauma-adjacent, or falsely specific — and it does so silently, without audit.

The question: what is the correct architecture for an atmosphere system that neither directs memory nor operates without governance?

---

## Decision

**Establish the Memory Atmosphere Engine as a governed contextual subsystem — a dependent consumer of the AI Context Broker and Governance Engine — that produces abstract, graduated atmosphere profiles rather than specific imagery, and that defaults toward stillness when confidence, stability, or safety conditions are not met.**

The engine is not an image generator. It produces an `AtmosphereProfile` — a structured package of presentation parameters (colour warmth, luminance, openness, motion level, transition speed, and so on) — and passes this to the presentation layer for rendering.

The engine cannot be in the retrieval chain. It cannot query the Content Layer, the Source table, or any governed material directly. All context arrives via the AI Context Broker.

---

## Rationale

### Why a subsystem, not a feature

Atmosphere governance intersects with privacy (what context may be inferred), safety (what imagery may be harmful), consent (what authenticated material may be shown), and cultural authority (what imagery is restricted by community protocol). These are not styling concerns. They require the same governance machinery applied to other AI outputs in LifeBook: minimum-data access, Context Broker mediation, audit trail, and explicit consent pathways for sensitive material.

A subsystem has named inputs, named outputs, defined relationships to other subsystems, and auditable behaviour. A feature does not.

### Why abstract atmosphere rather than imagery

The foundational principle — *LifeBook must never tell the participant what to remember* — has a specific technical implication: an image of a church placed in front of a participant discussing their faith is not neutral. It is a suggestion. It carries content. It may match their memory or it may not, and either way it has modified the conditions of recollection.

Abstract atmosphere — colour temperature, luminance, spatial openness — carries no specific content. It creates an emotional register without proposing a scene. The participant's own memory fills the space; the environment does not compete with it.

### Why topic inertia is architecturally required

Without inertia, the atmosphere system reacts to every noun. A participant saying "my daughter just called and wants to go golfing" mid-reflection on their wedding day would trigger an outdoor atmosphere change. This is wrong in every dimension: it destabilizes the environment, it privileges recent speech over settled context, and it subordinates cognition to keyword extraction.

Inertia is not a UX choice. It is a constraint on the engine's behaviour that follows directly from the foundational principle. If the environment chases keywords, the environment is directing memory.

### Why the engine defaults downward

Confidence and stability thresholds exist because the engine cannot be certain it has correctly identified the settled topic, the emotional register, or the sensitivity of the material being discussed. When certainty is low, the risk of producing an inappropriate atmosphere — one that is too specific, too evocative, or culturally inappropriate — is high. Defaulting to Level 0 (neutral) eliminates that risk. The cost is minimal: a neutral field is never harmful. A poorly inferred evocative image may be.

### Why trauma-sensitive behaviour is a hard constraint

A participant discussing a difficult memory is not inviting the system to visualize it for them. The mention of violence, death, medical crisis, or abuse does not constitute consent to depict those events. The engine's trauma response — immediate retreat to Level 0, no transition, no recovery until conditions clear — is not conservative styling. It is the only safe response to a category of content where the cost of error is psychological harm to a vulnerable participant.

## Consequences

### New document

`MEMORY_ATMOSPHERE_ENGINE.md v0.1` is the authoritative specification for this subsystem. It governs inputs, outputs, atmosphere levels (0–4), memory inertia rules, safety constraints, trauma-sensitive behaviour, governance and consent requirements, and relationships to existing subsystems.

### Changes to existing documents

The following documents require targeted additions to acknowledge the Memory Atmosphere Engine:

| Document | Change required |
|---|---|
| `ARCHITECTURE_FREEZE_V1.md` | Add Memory Atmosphere Engine to governed document register |
| `SCHEMA_INVENTORY.md` | Add deferred schema objects (AtmosphereProfile, AtmosphereConsentRecord, AtmospherePolicyVersion, AtmosphereAuditLog, ArtifactAtmospherePermission) to deferred register |
| `AI_CONTEXT_BROKER.md` | Document the engine as a registered downstream consumer of context signals; add `atmosphere_context` output to the broker's output surface |
| `CONTENT_LAYER.md` | Add reference to the engine as a downstream consumer of artifact and source authenticity signals |
| `OPERATIONAL_MODELS.md` | Add atmosphere-sensitive escalation trigger: `atmosphere_safety_retreat` — fires when the engine retreats to Level 0 during an active session due to a safety constraint, for steward notification |

### New architecture principle proposed

The following principle is proposed for addition to `ARCHITECTURE_FREEZE_V1.md` in the next revision session:

**P14 — Atmosphere is subordinate to cognition**  
The Memory Atmosphere Engine may only create conditions that support authentic memory. It may not direct, bias, dramatize, or substitute for recollection. Any feature, output, or configuration that violates this principle is prohibited regardless of technical feasibility or apparent user benefit.

### Deferred schema objects

Five schema objects are identified and documented in `MEMORY_ATMOSPHERE_ENGINE.md §11`. None are migration blockers for 0002. They require schema design review before any migration is produced.

### Agent registry addition (deferred)

A `memory_atmosphere_policy_evaluator` entry will be required in the AI Context Broker's Agent Registry (§3.2) when the engine is implemented. The name is deliberate: the subsystem evaluates governed policy inputs to produce an atmosphere profile. It does not retrieve material, authenticate evidence, establish facts, or choose imagery. It will operate under the `respectful_generation` Context Profile with a narrowed `max_data_categories` constraint excluding any content that carries `access_classification = restricted` or `culturally_governed`. Registration is deferred pending implementation.

### Implementation order

The Memory Atmosphere Engine must not be implemented before:

1. The AI Context Broker's context resolution pathway is operational
2. The Governance Engine can produce `governance_tier`, `personalization_permitted`, and artifact-level permissions
3. The accessibility preference system is operational and `prefers-reduced-motion` is reliably propagated
4. Participant consent infrastructure is in place (required before Level 4)

Level 4 (Authenticated Personal Atmosphere) must not be enabled until item 4 is satisfied and a separate Discovery Partner review session approves the consent model.

## Alternatives Considered

**Keyword-to-image mapping.** Rejected. Directs memory. No audit trail. No sensitivity handling. Violates the foundational principle.

**Generative image model as primary atmosphere source.** Rejected as primary source without governance scaffolding. Generative imagery without specificity constraints, inertia, sensitivity detection, and consent pathways produces output that cannot be governed. It may become a component of Level 3 or Level 4 in a future session, under explicit governance constraints, but it is not appropriate as the engine's primary mechanism.

**No atmosphere subsystem — rely entirely on product design.** Rejected. Atmosphere at scale requires consistent, auditable, governed behaviour across all sessions and languages. Leaving this to ad-hoc product design decisions produces inconsistent safety behaviour and no audit trail.

**Single static background.** Rejected. Does not serve participants whose emotional or cultural context would be mismatched by a fixed visual. Static-mode is preserved as a participant-selectable preference, not as the system default.

## Earned Atmosphere

The concept of earned atmosphere (documented in `MEMORY_ATMOSPHERE_ENGINE.md §13`) complements the existing governance, provenance, and confidence models rather than replacing them.

The relationship is additive and sequential: governance determines what material may be used; provenance establishes whether that material is authentic; confidence measures how well-evidenced the understanding is; earned atmosphere translates that accumulated authenticated understanding into a proportionally richer environment.

This means atmospheric richness cannot be manufactured, accelerated by product development, or unlocked by feature flags. It accumulates through the governed record — verified stories, contributed artifacts, established relationships — at the pace the participant's authentic history warrants. A newly created LifeBook begins restrained by design. The principle that every increase in atmospheric specificity must be justified by a corresponding increase in authenticated understanding is the architectural expression of this intent.
